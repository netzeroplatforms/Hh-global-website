01 Proposition
CityFi (LuxMetrix) is a 17-sensor retrofit unit that fits into the NEMA and Zhaga sockets already on every streetlight. No replacement of the column, no new cable runs, no civils. Activates the lighting network as a city-scale environmental and operational sensing grid. Patent pending. Data sold as a service. Owners of streetlight infrastructure (PFI operators, local authority highways teams) unlock a recurring data revenue stream from an asset they already paid for. Buyers of the data (smart city teams, air quality compliance leads) get persistent, high-resolution coverage without standing up new infrastructure.
Anchor stats: 17 sensors per unit · 6+ environmental sensor types · NEMA + Zhaga standard sockets · retrofit (no new infrastructure) · patent pending · data-as-a-service revenue model.

"Every City Already Has a Sensor Network"

Mark Evans-Smith authored — repost-friendly
Draft
Every city already has a sensor network. It's just not switched on. Around 7 million streetlights across the UK. Most installed in the last 15 years. Almost all of them sit on NEMA or Zhaga sockets — the standard sockets designed, originally, for smart lighting controls. Those same sockets accept a 17-sensor environmental unit. No new columns. No civils. No new cable. Drop it in, plug it in, the streetlight starts measuring air quality, footfall, traffic, noise, weather, microclimate. The data is sold as a service. The economics are obvious. The asset is paid for. The socket is already there. The only thing missing is the data layer. CityFi (BridgePoint AI portfolio) turns lighting infrastructure into a recurring revenue line for whoever owns it — PFI operators, local authorities, combined authorities — and gives smart city teams the density of data they've been quietly waiting for. Patent pending. Quietly piloting. Happy to talk to anyone running a lighting estate or buying environmental data. #smartcities #infrastructure #airquality #urbantech
Use: Mark posts as authored content. Targets PFI operators and smart city leads. Soft CTA at the end — "happy to talk" — invites inbound rather than chasing.
Post · Data-driven hook

"The Air Quality Data Problem (and Why It's a Streetlight Problem)"

Mark Evans-Smith authored
Draft
There are roughly 300 DEFRA reference air quality stations covering the whole of the UK. There are roughly 7 million streetlights. The mismatch tells you everything about why air quality data is so frustrating for the people who actually need it — local authorities under Environment Act obligations, planning teams running impact assessments, schools and hospitals trying to evidence exposure. Reference-grade is expensive and sparse. Indicative-grade is cheap but inconsistent. CityFi's bet (and ours at BridgePoint): the right unit of measurement is the streetlight. Not because it's clever — because it's already there. A 17-sensor unit that fits into the NEMA/Zhaga socket every modern streetlight has. Retrofit, not replace. Data as a service. The interesting question isn't "can it be done." It's "who owns the resulting data revenue stream when it is?" If you're running a lighting estate, that's a question worth having a view on. #smartcities #airquality #data #infrastructure
Use: Targets the "who owns the data" question — provocative, draws responses from PFI operators specifically (they have the strongest claim on the data revenue).
