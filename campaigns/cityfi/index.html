01 Proposition
CityFi (LuxMetrix) is a 17-sensor retrofit unit that fits into the NEMA and Zhaga sockets already on every streetlight. No replacement of the column, no new cable runs, no civils. Activates the lighting network as a city-scale environmental and operational sensing grid. Patent pending. Data sold as a service. Owners of streetlight infrastructure (PFI operators, local authority highways teams) unlock a recurring data revenue stream from an asset they already paid for. Buyers of the data (smart city teams, air quality compliance leads) get persistent, high-resolution coverage without standing up new infrastructure.
Anchor stats: 17 sensors per unit · 6+ environmental sensor types · NEMA + Zhaga standard sockets · retrofit (no new infrastructure) · patent pending · data-as-a-service revenue model.

Post 02 · Timing-locked

"Six weeks until the 30 June LAQM deadline"

Mark Evans-Smith authored · post within 7 days, expires after 30 June 2026
Ready
Six weeks until the 30 June LAQM deadline. Every council in England outside London has to submit a 2025 Annual Status Report to DEFRA's air quality portal. New this year: a mandatory "Year of Objective Compliance" projection. Councils have to say when they will actually hit air quality targets, not just where they are now. That projection is hard to make with sparse reference-station data. There are 630 DEFRA reference monitoring sites for the entire UK. The Environment Act PM2.5 target is measured as population exposure — which means knowing where people are, what they are breathing, and how it varies across a borough. You cannot answer that with point monitoring. The honest answer for most highways and environment teams right now is: we have the data we have. We will report what we can report. The compliance projection will be a guess. There is another option. 7 million streetlights. NEMA and Zhaga sockets. A 17-sensor retrofit unit (LuxMetrix, CityFi) that turns the lighting estate into a hyperlocal sensing network — without new infrastructure. If your team is currently writing a 2026 AQAP with a straight face, it is worth a 20-minute conversation. #airquality #LAQM #localauthorities #smartcities

Post 05 · Values frame

"Air quality is a justice issue"

Mark Evans-Smith authored · environmental justice / data poverty angle
Ready
Air quality is a justice issue. The Air Quality Expert Group's 2025 report on differentials in pollutant exposure confirmed what most public health teams already knew: ethnic minority communities in the UK are roughly three times more likely than white communities to live in high-pollution neighbourhoods. Royal College of Physicians estimates 30,000 air-pollution-attributable deaths a year. Which means it matters where the monitoring stations are. There are 630 DEFRA reference stations covering the whole UK. The Cambridge Environmental Data Science journal described the network in March 2025 as "spatially sparse with heterogeneous placement." Translation: the neighbourhoods with the worst pollution are also the neighbourhoods with the fewest measurements. Data poverty layered on top of exposure poverty. Same areas, both ways. You do not fix that by deploying more reference-grade stations — they cost too much per site to ever cover a city evenly. You fix it by using the network that is already there. 7 million streetlights. NEMA and Zhaga sockets. A 17-sensor retrofit unit and the lighting estate becomes the monitoring estate. CityFi (BridgePoint AI portfolio) exists to make that argument operationally real. Patent pending. Data sold as a service. Designed for the boroughs that get measured least. Worth a conversation if your authority's air quality strategy has been written with data you do not actually have. #airquality #environmentaljustice #smartcities #publichealth
Hook source: AQEG 2025 report "Differentials in air pollutant exposure"; Royal College of Physicians 30,000 deaths/yr figure; Cambridge Environmental Data Science (Mar 2025) on sparse network; HoC Library briefing CBP-9600 (Dec 2025). Mark Evans-Smith authored
Draft
