The honest position

The product and IP stack are credible: CE-marked, ISO 13485-compliant, EUDAMED registered, clinically tested across 200+ patients, and supported by a patented Stoma Stick device and a 493-class diagnostic ontology. The current constraint is execution. UK deployment is blocked by missing MHRA progress, unresolved IP assignment confirmation, non-UK hosting, phone-based demo authentication, and the absence of real UK traction.

Working thesisManufacturer-first remains the most commercially sensible entry route, with NHS validation running in parallel as the credibility multiplier. The Facebook community and charity relationships are the fastest evidence engine.

Critical messaging correction

The platform should be described as semi-automated clinical decision support, not fully automated image classification. The current logic depends on clinician-selected features and then suggests complications. That is the safe, diligence-ready framing and reduces avoidable credibility risk in NHS and acquirer conversations.

Decision lens for BridgePointAI

What has to be true

Proceed conditionally if:

IP assignment documents can be produced quickly.
MHRA work has an owner and a dated plan.

Walk away if:

Terms are deferred while strategic value is extracted now.

Audience-weighted decision view

Manufacturers: still the fastest monetisation path at 6–9 months because the threat of patient switching and the value of real-world outcomes data are immediate.

NHS / ICBs: remain the slower 18–24 month credibility route, best entered via SBRI rather than direct procurement.

Patients: are not the primary revenue engine but are now even more important as the evidence engine, with leak detection and confidence support as the sharpest value props.

Live call prep

Use this in the meeting — check off as answered
1. MHRA registration status

Has the process started, who owns it, and what date are they working to?

Good answer: named consultant, submission stage and completion date.
Red flag: “we know we need to do it” or expectation that BridgePointAI will handle it.
2. IP ownership structure

Can they show assignment documentation from PHIN Consulting PL to PHIN MED UK?

Good answer: signed agreement can be shared immediately.
Red flag: “it is being sorted” or any hesitation.
3. Demo environment authentication

What is the delivery date for replacing phone sign-in with email or link access, and who is building it?

Good answer: named developer and sprint date.
Red flag: “it is on the backlog”.
4. AI classification accuracy

Can they clearly describe what the AI does and does not do in production?

Good answer: semi-automated decision support, not auto-classification.
Red flag: overclaiming image AI capability.
5. Previous consultant settlement

Are all prior agreements terminated, billing resolved and no residual equity claims outstanding?

Good answer: written closure evidence exists.
Red flag: ambiguous or unresolved legacy claims.
6. Grant applications

Have they applied to SBRI, NIHR i4i or Innovate UK, and if not, why not?

Good answer: active application calendar.
Red flag: no bandwidth or no awareness.
7. UK clinical study timeline

When is ethics submission and which UK site is the lead site?

Good answer: named trust and dated plan.
Red flag: protocol exists but nothing has moved.
8. Equity expectations

What does PHIN MED actually expect from a BridgePointAI engagement?

Good answer: specific position or milestone-based structure.
Red flag: “we can discuss that later”.

UK build blueprint

Using the master Notion structure to turn the call brief into an execution frame
How this enhances the HTMLThe Notion-ready structure adds a fuller operating model behind the current call brief. It shows that Stoma Alert is not just a regulatory and outreach story; it is also a company-build, diligence, migration, governance, support and launch-readiness programme. That makes the HTML more useful for investors, advisors and delivery partners.

1. Establish the UK company properly

Fold company setup, governance, DPO, Clinical Safety Officer, insurance, incident reporting and board design into the call narrative so questions about readiness cover the business shell as well as the product.

2. Treat UK + Poland as one regulated transition

Extend the current MHRA / DCB focus into a combined Poland-to-UK integration plan: document translation, CE review, technical file rebuild, UK labelling and evidence suitability.

3. Add platform-history diligence

Use the historical platform review to ask for code repos, architecture, UI files, Jira boards, clinical studies and legacy marketing assets. This sharpens the IP and evidence questions already in the call prep.

4. Expand the product review lens

Current HTML covers SSO, hosting and AI claims. The master structure adds code quality, API review, accessibility, security audit, database review and website claims compliance.

5. Add cloud and service management

Move from “UK cloud migration” to a fuller hosting plan: architecture, IAM, encryption, backup, monitoring, SLA, change control and audit-readiness. This makes the platform more acquisition-ready.

6. Connect to commercial launch

Support setup, partner onboarding, benchmarking, GTM channels, market sizing and the final build checklist can all be surfaced as downstream workstreams under the existing 6‑month pathway.
