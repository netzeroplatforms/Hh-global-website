Post 1 · Awaab's Law / Forensic Mycology · v1.1 thought-leadership rewrite CO PAGE · WK 02 MON · GATED
Awaab's Law has imported the standards of court-grade science into a market that, until recently, was operating on building-surveyor norms. That is the structural change worth naming. CPR Part 35 expects an expert report to: — state the question asked — state the methodology used — state the limits of confidence — state the alternative explanations considered A report missing any of those four is not a CPR-35 report. It is a surveyor opinion with the wrong label on it. In a contested instruction under Awaab's Law, the difference matters. This is a market-wide observation, not a sales pitch. The housing-disrepair sector needs to absorb that the evidential bar has moved — supplier choice should follow that, not lead it. — HH Global Holdings · Industry observation

Post 4 · Sam founder voice — Awaab's Law · v1.1 thought-leadership rewrite SAM PERSONAL · WK 02 TUE · GATED
Across many damp and mould reports I have reviewed as an expert witness, the single most common failure is not in the testing. It is in one paragraph — the "alternative explanations" section. A judge does not need to be convinced that mould was present. They need to be convinced that the expert considered, and properly excluded, every other plausible cause of the tenant's harm — and that the methodology was capable of doing so. If the report does not contain that paragraph, the case is wobbly. If it contains the paragraph but the methodology was not built to do that work, the case collapses on cross. That is the gap Awaab's Law has exposed at sector scale. It is a methodology gap, not a testing gap, and it is going to take this sector several years to close. This is the discipline that matters now. Worth saying publicly. — Sam

There is a model of scientific business that does not get talked about enough.
